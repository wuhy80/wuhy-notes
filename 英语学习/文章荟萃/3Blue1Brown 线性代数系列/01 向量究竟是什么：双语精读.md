## 三种视角看向量（Interpretations of Vectors）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| The fundamental building block for linear algebra is the vector, so it's worth making sure we're all on the same page about _what_ exactly a vector _is_. | 线性代数的基本构件（building block）是向量（vector），所以值得先确认一下：关于*向量究竟*是*什么*，我们大家想的都是同一回事。 |
| You see, broadly speaking there are three distinct-but-related interpretations of vectors, which I'll call the physics student perspective, the computer science perspective, and the mathematician's perspective. | 你看，大体上讲，向量有三种「彼此不同但又相互关联」的理解方式，我把它们分别称为物理学系学生的视角、计算机科学家的视角和数学家的视角。 |
| **Physics Perspective** | **物理学视角** |
| The physics student perspective is that vectors are arrows pointing in space. What defines a given vector is its length and the direction it's pointing, but as long as those two facts are the same you can move it around and it's still the same vector. | 物理学系学生的视角是：向量是空间中指向某处的箭头（arrow）。决定一个向量的，是它的长度和它指向的方向；只要这两点相同，你把它挪来挪去，它都还是同一个向量。 |
| Vectors that live in a flat plane are two-dimensional, and those sitting in the broader space that we live in are three-dimensional. | 躺在一张平面上的向量是二维的（two-dimensional），而待在我们生活的这个更广阔空间里的向量则是三维的（three-dimensional）。 |
| **CS Perspective** | **计算机科学视角** |
| The computer science perspective is that vectors are ordered lists of numbers. For example, if you were doing some analytics about house prices, and the only features you cared about were square footage and price, you might model each house as a pair of numbers, the first indicating square footage, and the second indicating price. | 计算机科学的视角是：向量是有序的数字列表（ordered list of numbers）。比如说，假如你在做一些房价分析，而你在意的特征只有面积和价格这两个，那你可能会把每栋房子建模成一对数字：第一个数表示面积（平方英尺），第二个数表示价格。 |

$$
\left[\begin{array}{l}
2,600 \mathrm{ft}^2 \\
\$ 300,000
\end{array}\right]
$$

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| Notice that order matters here. In the lingo, you'd be modeling houses as two-dimensional vectors, where "vector" is pretty much a fancy word for list, and what makes it two-dimensional is the fact that its length is two. | 注意，这里的顺序是有讲究的。用行话说，你这是把房子建模成了二维向量——在这里「向量」基本上就是「列表」的花哨说法，而所谓二维，指的就是它的长度为 2。 |
| **Mathematician's Perspective** | **数学家视角** |
| The mathematician generalizes both of these views, basically saying that a vector can be anything where there's a sensible notion of adding two vectors and multiplying a vector by a number, operations that we'll talk about later in this chapter. | 数学家把上面两种观点都做了推广，大致是说：只要「把两个向量相加」和「把向量乘以一个数」这两个运算有合理的定义，向量就可以是任何东西。这两个运算我们会在这章后面谈到。 |
| The details of this view are rather abstract, and it can be healthy to ignore it until later in this series, favoring a more concrete setting in the interim. The reason we bring it up here is that it hints at the fact that the ideas of vector addition and multiplication by numbers will play an important role throughout these topics. | 这种观点的细节相当抽象；在系列进行到后面之前先把它放一放、暂时待在一个更具体的场景里，反而是件健康的事。我们在这里提它的原因，是它暗示了一件事实：向量加法与数乘（multiplication by numbers）这两个想法，会在这些主题中全程扮演重要角色。 |

## 思考坐标系（Thinking About Coordinate Systems）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| Now, while many of you are already familiar with coordinate systems, it's worth walking through them explicitly since this is where all the important back and forth between the two main perspectives of linear algebra happens. Focusing our attention on two dimensions for the moment, you have a horizontal line, called the _x-axis_, and a vertical line, called the _y-axis_. | 好，虽然你们许多人早就熟悉坐标系（coordinate system）了，但还是值得把它明确过一遍，因为线性代数两大主要视角之间所有重要的来回转换，都发生在这里。眼下先把注意力放在二维：你有一条水平线，叫 _x-axis_（$x$ 轴），还有一条竖直线，叫 _y-axis_（$y$ 轴）。 |
| The place where they intersect is the _origin_, which you should think of as the center of space and the root of all vectors. | 两条轴相交的地方叫 _origin_（原点）。你应该把它想成空间的中心、所有向量的根。 |
| After choosing an arbitrary distance to represent a length of $1$, you make tick marks on each axis spaced out by this distance. | 任选一段距离来代表长度 $1$ 之后，你在每条轴上按这段距离的间隔刻上刻度（tick marks）。 |
| When we want to convey the idea of 2d space as a whole, which comes up a lot in this text, we'll extend these tick marks to make grid lines, like so: | 当我们想表达「二维空间作为一个整体」这个概念时——本文里这种情形会反复出现——我们就把这些刻度延伸成网格线（grid lines），就像这样： |
| Let's settle on a specific thought to have in mind when we say the word _vector_. Given the geometric focus we're shooting for here, whenever we introduce a new topic involving vectors, we want you to first think about an arrow, and specifically think about an arrow inside a coordinate system, like the $xy$-plane, with its tail sitting at the origin. | 来定下一个具体的念头，作为听到 _vector_ 这个词时该想到的画面。鉴于我们这里追求的是几何直观，以后每引入一个涉及向量的新话题，都希望你首先想到一支箭头——而且是坐标系（比如 $xy$ 平面）里的一支箭头，箭尾（tail）就坐在原点上。 |
| The coordinates of a vector are a pair of numbers that basically give instructions for how to get from the tail of that vector at the origin, to its tip. The first number tells you how far to walk along the $x$-axis, with positive numbers indicating rightward motion and negative numbers indicating leftward motion, and the second number tells you how far to then walk parallel to the $y$-axis, with positive numbers indicating upward motion, and negative numbers indicating downward motion. | 向量的坐标（coordinates）是一对数，它基本上就是一套「从原点处的箭尾走到箭尖（tip）」的行走指令。第一个数告诉你沿 $x$ 轴走多远——正数表示向右，负数表示向左；第二个数告诉你接着平行于 $y$ 轴走多远——正数表示向上，负数表示向下。 |
| To distinguish vectors from points, the convention is to write this pair of numbers vertically with square brackets around them. Every pair of numbers gives you one _and only one_ vector, and every vector is associated with one _and only one_ pair of numbers. | 为了把向量和点区分开，惯例是把这对数竖着写，外面套上方括号。每一对数给你一个*并且仅有这一个*向量；每一个向量，也只对应*唯一*的一对数。 |
| The vector that corresponds with walking $4$ units down and then $7$ units to the right is $\left[\begin{array}{c}7 \\ -4\end{array}\right]$, because $4$ units down is negative four on the $y$-axis and $7$ units to the right is positive seven on the $x$-axis. | （译注：原文此处是一道互动测验的答案解析，题目在官网原页面上——先向下走 4 格、再向右走 7 格，对应哪个向量。）向下走 $4$ 格再向右走 $7$ 格，对应的向量是 $\left[\begin{array}{c}7 \\ -4\end{array}\right]$：向下 4 格对应 $y$ 轴上的负四，向右 7 格对应 $x$ 轴上的正七。 |
| In three-dimensions, you add a third axis, called the $z$-axis, which is perpendicular to both the $x$ and the $y$ axes. In this case, each vector is associated with an ordered triplet of numbers: the first number tells you how far to move along the $x$-axis, the second number tells you how far to move parallel to the $y$-axis, and the third number tells you how far to move parallel to the new $z$-axis. | 到了三维，你要再加第三条轴，叫 $z$ 轴（$z$-axis），它同时垂直于 $x$ 轴和 $y$ 轴。这时，每个向量对应一个有序的三元数组（triplet）：第一个数告诉你沿 $x$ 轴移动多远，第二个数告诉你平行于 $y$ 轴移动多远，第三个数告诉你平行于新 $z$ 轴移动多远。 |
| Every triplet of numbers gives you one unique vector in space, and every vector in space gives you exactly one triplet of numbers. | 每一组三元数组给你空间中唯一一个向量，空间中的每一个向量也恰好对应唯一一组三元数组。 |

## 向量运算（Vector Operations）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| So what about vector addition, and multiplying numbers by vectors? After all, every topic in linear algebra centers around these two operations. Luckily, these are both relatively straight-forward. | 那么，向量加法和「数乘向量」又是怎么回事？毕竟，线性代数的每个主题都围绕着这两个运算。幸运的是，它们都相当直白。 |
| **Addition** | **加法** |
| Let's say we have two vectors, one pointing up and a little to the right, and another pointing to the right and a little bit down. | 假设我们有两个向量：一个指向上方、略偏右，另一个指向右方、略微朝下。 |
| To add these two vectors, move the second vector so that it's tail sits on the tip of the first one. Then if you draw a new vector from the tail of the first one to where the tip of the second now sits, that new vector is their sum. | 要把这两个向量相加，先移动第二个向量，让它的箭尾坐在第一个向量的箭尖上。然后从第一个向量的箭尾出发，画一个新向量，连到第二个向量箭尖此刻所在的位置——这个新向量就是它们的和。 |
| Why is this a reasonable thing to do? Why this definition of addition and not some other? Well, one way to think about it is that each vector represents a certain movement; a step with a certain distance and direction. If you take a step along the first vector, then take a step in the direction and distance described by the second vector, the overall effect is the same as if it just moved along the sum of those two vectors. | 为什么这样做是合理的？为什么加法的定义是这样而不是别的样？有一个思路是：每个向量都代表一种确定的运动——一次带有特定距离和方向的跨步。如果你沿着第一个向量迈一步，再按第二个向量所描述的方向和距离迈一步，整体效果就和直接沿着这两个向量之和移动一次完全一样。 |
| You could think of this as an extension of how we think about adding numbers on a number line. One of the ways we teach kids to think about addition, say $2+5$, is to think of moving $2$ steps to the right, followed by another $5$ steps to the right. The overall effect is the same as if you just took $7$ steps to the right. | 你可以把它看成「在数轴上想加法」这一思路的延伸。我们教小孩子算加法（比如 $2+5$）的方式之一，就是先向右走 2 步，再向右走 5 步——整体效果与你直接向右走 7 步相同。 |
| In fact, let's see how vector addition looks numerically. The first vector here has coordinates $\left[\begin{array}{l}1 \\ 2\end{array}\right]$, and the second has coordinates $\left[\begin{array}{c}3 \\ -1\end{array}\right]$. When you take their vector sum using this tip to tail method, you can think of a four step path from the tail of the first to the tip of the second: Walk $1$ to the right, then $2$ up, then $3$ to the right, then $1$ down. | 我们干脆用数字来看看向量加法长什么样。这里第一个向量的坐标是 $\left[\begin{array}{l}1 \\ 2\end{array}\right]$，第二个的坐标是 $\left[\begin{array}{c}3 \\ -1\end{array}\right]$。用这种「箭尾接箭尖」（tip to tail）的方法取向量和时，你可以想成一条四步路径：向右走 1，向上走 2，再向右走 3，最后向下走 1。 |
| Reorganizing these steps so that you first do all the rightward motion, then all the vertical motion, you can read it as saying first move $1+3$ to the right, then move $2-1$ up. So the new vector has coordinates $4$ and $1$. | 把这些步骤重新排一下，先走完全部水平移动、再走全部竖直移动，就可以读成：先向右移 $1+3$，再向上移 $2-1$。于是新向量的坐标就是 4 和 1。 |
| In general, to add two vectors in the list-of-numbers conception of vectors, match up their terms and add them each together. | 一般来说，在「向量即数字列表」的观念下，两个向量相加就是把对应的项配对、逐个相加。 |

$$
\left[\begin{array}{l}
x_1 \\
y_1
\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}{l}
x_2 \\
y_2
\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{l}
x_1+x_2 \\
y_1+y_2
\end{array}\right]
$$

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| $\begin{bmatrix}4\\-2\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}6\\2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}10\\0\end{bmatrix}$ so we only need to walk $10$ units along the positive $x$-axis. | （译注：原文此处是一道互动测验的答案解析，题目在官网原页面上。）$\begin{bmatrix}4\\-2\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}6\\2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}10\\0\end{bmatrix}$，所以我们只需沿 $x$ 轴正方向走 10 个单位。 |
| **Scaling** | **缩放** |
| The other fundamental vector operation is multiplication by a number. This is best understood by just looking at a few examples. If you take the number $2$, and multiply it by a given vector, you stretch out that vector so that it's two times as long as when you started. | 另一个基本的向量运算是「乘以一个数」。理解它最好的方式，就是直接看几个例子。取数字 $2$，让它去乘一个给定的向量，你就会把这个向量拉长（stretch）到开始时长度的两倍。 |
| If you multiply a vector by $\frac13$, you squish it down so that it is one-third its original length. | 如果你把向量乘以 $\frac13$，就会把它压扁（squish）到原来长度的三分之一。 |
| If you multiply it by a negative number, like $-1.5$, then the vector gets flipped around, then stretched out by a factor of $1.5$. | 如果你乘的是一个负数，比如 $-1.5$，向量就会先被翻转方向，再拉长到 1.5 倍。 |
| This process of stretching, squishing, and sometimes reversing direction, is called "scaling." Whenever you catch a number like $2$, $\frac13$, or $-1.5$ acting like this, scaling some vector, you call it a "scalar". In fact, throughout linear algebra, one of the main things numbers do is scale vectors, so it's common to use the word _scalar_ interchangeably with the word _number_. Numerically, stretching out a vector by a factor of $2$ corresponds with multiplying each of its coordinates by $2$. So in the conception of vectors as lists of numbers, multiplying a given vector by a scalar means multiplying each one of its components by that scalar. | 这种拉伸、压扁、有时还反转方向的过程，就叫「缩放」（scaling）。每当你看到某个数——比如 $2$、$\frac13$ 或 $-1.5$——正在这样摆弄某个向量，你就叫它「标量」（scalar）。实际上，纵观整个线性代数，数字干的主要事情之一就是缩放向量，所以人们常把 _scalar_ 这个词和 _number_ 混用。从数字上看，把向量拉长到 2 倍，对应着把它的每个坐标都乘以 2。所以在「向量即数字列表」的观念下，用一个标量乘给定向量，意思就是把它的每个分量（component）都乘以这个标量。 |

$$
2 \vec{\mathbf{v}}=2 \cdot\left[\begin{array}{l}
x \\
y
\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{l}
2 x \\
2 y
\end{array}\right]
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{3} \left[\begin{array}{c} 12 \\ 9 \end{array}\right] &= \left[\begin{array}{c} 12/3 \\ 9/3 \end{array}\right] \\ \rule{0pt}{2.0em}
&=\left[\begin{array}{c} 4 \\ 3 \end{array}\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

## 结语（Conclusion）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| You'll see in the following chapters what we mean when we say pretty much every linear algebra topic revolves around these two fundamental operations: vector addition and scalar multiplication. We'll also talk more in the last linear algebra chapter about how and why the mathematician thinks _only_ about these operations, independent and abstracted away from however you choose to represent vectors. | 在接下来的章节里，你就会明白我们说「几乎所有线性代数主题都围绕着这两个基本运算——向量加法和标量乘法」时，是什么意思。在最后一章线性代数里，我们还会进一步聊聊：数学家*为什么*、又如何*只*盯着这两个运算来思考——完全独立于、超脱于你选择用哪种方式来表示向量。 |
| In truth, it doesn't matter whether you think of vectors as fundamentally being arrows in space that happen to have a nice numerical representation, or fundamentally as lists of numbers that happen to have a nice geometric interpretation. The usefulness of linear algebra has less to do with either one of these views than it does with the ability to translate back and forth between them. It gives the data-analyst a nice way to conceptualize many lists of numbers in a visual way, which can seriously clarify patterns in the data and give a global view of what certain operations do. | 说实话，无论你认为向量的本质是空间中的箭头、恰好拥有漂亮的数值表示，还是本质是数字列表、恰好拥有漂亮的几何解释，都不打紧。线性代数之所以有用，与其说在于这两种看法中的任何一种，不如说在于能在两者之间来回翻译的能力。它给了数据分析师一种把一长串数字列表可视化地组织起来的好办法，能实实在在地澄清数据中的模式，并让你一眼看清某些运算在全局上做了什么。 |
| On the flip side, it gives people like physicists and computer graphics programmers a language to describe space, and the manipulation of space, using numbers that can be crunched and run through a computer. When one does mathy animations, for example, they start by thinking about what's going on in space, then get the computer to represent things numerically, and figure out where to place which pixels on the screen, and doing that often relies on an understanding of linear algebra. | 反过来，它又给了物理学家、计算机图形程序员这类人一门语言，用可以被计算机处理运算的数字去描述空间、操纵空间。比如说，做数学味儿的动画时，人们先思考空间里正在发生什么，然后让计算机把一切数字化地表示出来，算出屏幕上哪个位置该放哪个像素——而这往往就依赖于对线性代数的理解。 |
