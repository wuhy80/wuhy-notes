## 作为向量的函数（Functions as vectors）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| To build up to where this is going, let's spend the bulk of this lesson talking about something which is neither an arrow nor a list of numbers but also has vector-ish qualities: Functions. | 为了铺垫到我们要去的地方，本讲的大部分时间我们来谈谈一种既不是箭头也不是数字列表、却同样带有向量味的东西：函数（functions）。 |
| You see, there's a sense in which functions are just another type of vector. In the same way that we can add two vectors together, there's also a sensible notion for adding two functions $f$ and $g$ to get a new function, $f+g$. | 你看，在某种意义上，函数只是另一种类型的向量。正如我们可以把两个向量相加，把两个函数 $f$ 和 $g$ 相加得到新函数 $f+g$，也有一个讲得通的定义。 |

$$
(f + g)(x) = f(x) + g(x)
$$

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| This is one of those expressions that take a moment to parse. The output of the new function $(f + g)$ at any given input $x$, say $x = -4$, is the sum of the outputs of $f$ and $g$ when you evaluate them each at that same input. | 这属于那种要花点时间才能读懂的表达式。新函数 $(f + g)$ 在任何给定输入 $x$ 处的输出——比如说 $x = -4$——是 $f$ 与 $g$ 各自在同一输入处取值后输出的和。 |
| More generally, the value of the sum function at any input $x$ is the sum of the values of $f$ and $g$ at that input $x$. This is very similar to adding vectors coordinate-by-coordinate. It's just that there are, in a sense, infinitely many coordinates to deal with. | 更一般地，和函数在任意输入 $x$ 处的值，就是 $f$ 与 $g$ 在该输入 $x$ 处的值的和。这与把向量逐坐标相加非常相似，只不过从某种意义上说，要处理的坐标有无穷多个。 |
| Very similar to adding vectors: $\left[\begin{array}{l}x_1 \\ y_1 \\ z_1\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}{l}x_2 \\ y_2 \\ z_2\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{l}x_1+x_2 \\ y_1+y_2 \\ z_1+z_2\end{array}\right]$ | （图注）非常类似于把向量相加：$\left[\begin{array}{l}x_1 \\ y_1 \\ z_1\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}{l}x_2 \\ y_2 \\ z_2\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{l}x_1+x_2 \\ y_1+y_2 \\ z_1+z_2\end{array}\right]$ |
| Similarly, there's a sensible notion for scaling a function by a real number: Just scale all of its outputs by that number. Again, this is very analogous to scaling a vector coordinate-by-coordinate. | 类似地，用实数去缩放一个函数也有讲得通的定义：把它的所有输出都按这个数缩放就行。同样，这与逐坐标缩放向量非常类似。 |
| Very similar to scaling a vector: $2 \cdot\left[\begin{array}{l}x \\ y \\ z\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{l}2 x \\ 2 y \\ 2 z\end{array}\right]$ | （图注）非常类似于缩放向量：$2 \cdot\left[\begin{array}{l}x \\ y \\ z\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{l}2 x \\ 2 y \\ 2 z\end{array}\right]$ |
| Now, given that the only thing vectors can really do is get added together or scaled, it feels like we should be able to take all the same useful constructs and problem-solving techniques of linear algebra that we originally thought of in terms of arrows in space, and apply them to functions as well. | 既然向量真正能做的事情无非是相加或缩放，那么感觉上，我们应该能把线性代数中所有那些原本用「空间中的箭头」来想的实用构造和解题技巧，同样应用到函数上。 |
| For example, there's a perfectly reasonable notion of a linear transformation for functions. One familiar example comes from calculus: The derivative. It's something that transforms one function into another function. | 比如，对函数来说也存在一个完全合理的线性变换概念。一个熟悉的例子来自微积分：导数（derivative）。它把一个函数变成另一个函数。 |
| The derivative can be expressed as a linear transformation: $L\left(\frac{1}{9} x^3-x\right)=\frac{1}{3} x^2-1$ | （图注）导数可以表示为一个线性变换：$L\left(\frac{1}{9} x^3-x\right)=\frac{1}{3} x^2-1$ |
| Sometimes in this context of functions you'll hear these called "operators" instead of "transformations", but the meaning is the same. A natural question you might ask is what it means for a transformation of functions to be "linear". | 在函数的语境里，你有时会听到这些东西被称为「算子（operators）」而不是「变换」，但意思相同。你可能会自然地问：一个函数的变换是「线性的」，这是什么意思？ |

## 线性的标准定义（Standard definition of linear）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| In chapter 3 we offered a geometric constraint that characterizes linear transformations: They transform lines into lines, keep grid lines evenly spaced, and fix the origin in place. | 在第三章里，我们给出过一个刻画线性变换的几何约束：它们把直线变成直线、保持网格线均匀分布、让原点固定在原地。 |
| The true formal definition of linearity is more abstract, but the reward of the abstract definition is that we'll get something general enough to apply to functions as well as arrows, and ultimately to a much broader set of mathematical objects that deserve to be called vectors. | 线性性（linearity）真正形式化的定义更抽象，但抽象定义的回报是：我们得到的东西足够一般，既能应用到箭头上，也能应用到函数上，并最终应用到更广的一大批配得上「向量」之名的数学对象上。 |
| A transformation $L$ is _linear_ if for any two vectors $\vec{\mathbf{v}}$ and $\vec{\mathbf{w}}$, and any scalar $s$, the following two equations are true: | 如果对任意两个向量 $\vec{\mathbf{v}}$ 与 $\vec{\mathbf{w}}$、任意标量 $s$，下面两条方程都成立，那么变换 $L$ 就是*线性*的： |

$$
L(\vec{\mathbf{v}} + \vec{\mathbf{w}}) = L(\vec{\mathbf{v}}) + L(\vec{\mathbf{w}})
$$

$$
L(s\vec{\mathbf{v}}) = sL(\vec{\mathbf{v}})
$$

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| The first equation says that when you add any two vectors $\vec{\mathbf{v}}$ and $\vec{\mathbf{w}}$, then apply the transformation to their sum, you get the same result as if you add the transformed versions of $\vec{\mathbf{v}}$ and $\vec{\mathbf{w}}$. | 第一条方程说：把任意两个向量 $\vec{\mathbf{v}}$ 与 $\vec{\mathbf{w}}$ 相加、再对它们的和应用变换，得到的结果与「先变换再相加」相同。 |
| The second property says that when you scale a vector $\vec{\mathbf{v}}$ by some number $s$, then apply the transformation to the result, you get the same ultimate vector as if you scale the transformed version of $\vec{\mathbf{v}}$ by that same amount $s$. | 第二条性质说：把向量 $\vec{\mathbf{v}}$ 按某个数 $s$ 缩放、再对结果应用变换，最终得到的向量与「先变换再按同一量 $s$ 缩放」相同。 |
| The way you'll often hear this described is that linear transformations "preserve" the operations of vector addition and scalar-vector multiplication. The idea of grid lines remaining parallel and evenly spaced that we've talked about before is really just an illustration of what these two properties mean in the specific case of points in 2d space. | 你常会听到的说法是：线性变换「保持」（preserve）向量加法与标量-向量乘法这两种运算。我们之前讲过的「网格线保持平行且均匀分布」，其实只是这两条性质在二维空间点这一具体情形下的一个图示。 |
| One of the most important consequences of these properties, which makes matrix-vector multiplication possible, is that a linear transformation is completely described by where it takes the basis vectors. Since any vector can be expressed by scaling and adding the basis vectors in some way, finding the transformed version of a vector comes down to scaling and adding the transformed basis vectors in that same way. As you'll see in just a moment, this is as true for functions as it is for arrows. | 这些性质最重要的推论之一——它让矩阵-向量乘法成为可能——是：线性变换完全由它把基向量带到哪里来描述。既然任何向量都能表示成按某种方式缩放并相加基向量，求一个向量的变换版本，就归结为按同样的方式缩放并相加变换后的基向量。你马上会看到，这对函数和对箭头同样成立。 |

> [!note]- 词汇注释 · 线性的标准定义
> - **constraint** /kənˈstreɪnt/ —— 约束条件。a geometric constraint that characterizes linear transformations。
> - **characterize** /ˈkærəktəraɪz/ —— 刻画、表征。that characterizes linear transformations。
> - **linearity** /ˌlɪniˈærəti/ —— 线性（性）。The true formal definition of linearity。
> - **preserve** /prɪˈzɜːv/ —— 保持。linear transformations "preserve" the operations。
> - **ultimate** /ˈʌltɪmət/ —— 最终的。the same ultimate vector。
> - *drill in* —— 反复敲实、灌进脑子。To really drill in the parallel。
> - *come down to* —— 归结为。finding the transformed version of a vector comes down to scaling and adding。

## 作为线性变换的导数（Derivative as linear transformation）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| Calculus students are always using the fact that the derivative is additive and has the scaling property, even if they've never heard it phrased that way. If you add two functions, then take the derivative, it's the same as first taking the derivative of each separately and adding the results. | 学微积分的学生总是在用「导数可加且具有缩放性质」这一事实，哪怕他们从没听过这样的表述。把两个函数相加再求导，等同于先分别求导再把结果相加。 |
| Similarly, if you scale a function then take the derivative, it's the same as first taking the derivative then scaling the result. | 类似地，先缩放一个函数再求导，等同于先求导再缩放结果。 |
| To really drill in the parallel, let's show one way to describe the derivative with a matrix. This will be a little tricky, since function-spaces have a tendency to be infinite-dimensional, but the exercise is actually quite satisfying. | 为了把这个类比真正敲实，我们来展示一种用矩阵描述导数的方法。这会有点棘手，因为函数空间往往无穷维，但这个练习实际上相当令人满足。 |
| Let's limit ourselves to polynomials, things like $x^2+3x+5$ or $4x^7-5x^2$. Each polynomial can only have finitely many terms, but our full space of polynomials will include polynomials with arbitrarily large degrees. | 让我们把自己限制在多项式（polynomials）上，比如 $x^2+3x+5$ 或 $4x^7-5x^2$ 这样的东西。每个多项式只能有有限多项，但我们完整的多项式空间会包含次数任意大的多项式。 |
| The first thing we need to do is give coordinates to this space, which requires choosing a basis. Since polynomials are already expressed as the sum of scaled powers of $x$, it's pretty natural to choose pure powers of $x$ as basis functions. In other words, the first basis function is the constant function $b_0(x) = 1$, the second basis function is $b_1(x) = x$, then $b_2(x) = x^2$, then $b_3(x) = x^3$, and so on. | 我们要做的第一件事是给这个空间配上坐标，这需要选一个基。既然多项式本来就已经表示成 $x$ 的幂的缩放之和，很自然的选择就是把 $x$ 的纯幂作为基函数（basis functions）。换句话说，第一个基函数是常函数 $b_0(x) = 1$，第二个基函数是 $b_1(x) = x$，然后 $b_2(x) = x^2$，再然后 $b_3(x) = x^3$，依此类推。 |
| The role these functions serve will be similar to the roles of $\hat{\imath}$, $\hat{\jmath}$ and $\hat{k}$ in the world of vectors as arrows. Since our polynomials can have arbitrarily large degrees, this set of basis functions is infinite, but that's okay, it just means vectors have infinitely many coordinates. A polynomial like $x^2+3x+5$ would be described with the coordinates $\left[\begin{array}{c} 5 \\ 3 \\ 1 \\ \vdots \end{array}\right]$, then infinitely many zeros. | 这些函数扮演的角色，将类似于 $\hat{\imath}$、$\hat{\jmath}$ 和 $\hat{k}$ 在「向量即箭头」的世界里的角色。既然我们的多项式可以有任意大的次数，这组基函数是无穷的，但没关系，这只不过意味着向量有无穷多个坐标。像 $x^2+3x+5$ 这样的多项式，会用坐标 $\left[\begin{array}{c} 5 \\ 3 \\ 1 \\ \vdots \end{array}\right]$ 来描述，后面跟着无穷多个零。 |
| You'd read this as saying it's $5$ times the first basis function, plus $3$ times the second basis function plus $1$ times the third basis function, then none of the other basis functions should be added from that point on. | 你可以把它读作：它是第一个基函数的 $5$ 倍，加上第二个基函数的 $3$ 倍，加上第三个基函数的 $1$ 倍，从那往后不再添加任何其他基函数。 |
| The polynomial $4x^7-5x^2$ would have coordinates: | 多项式 $4x^7-5x^2$ 的坐标则是： |

$$
\left[\begin{array}{c} 0 \\ 0 \\ -5 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 4 \\ \vdots \end{array}\right]
$$

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| then infinitely many zeros to follow. In general, since every individual polynomial has only finitely many terms, its coordinates will be some finite string of numbers with an infinite tail of zeros. | 后面跟着无穷多个零。一般地，既然每个具体的多项式只有有限多项，它的坐标就是一串有限的数字、带上一条无穷的零尾巴。 |

> [!note]- 词汇注释 · 作为线性变换的导数
> - **additive** /ˈædɪtɪv/ —— 可加的。the derivative is additive。
> - **polynomial** /ˌpɒləˈnəʊmiəl/ —— 多项式。Let's limit ourselves to polynomials。
> - **degree** /dɪˈɡriː/ —— 次数。polynomials with arbitrarily large degrees。
> - **finite** /ˈfaɪnaɪt/ —— 有限的。finitely many terms。
> - **arbitrarily** /ˈɑːbɪtrərəli/ —— 任意地。arbitrarily large degrees。
> - **satisfying** /ˈsætɪsfaɪɪŋ/ —— 令人满足的。the exercise is actually quite satisfying。
> - *limit ourselves to* —— 把自己限制在……。Let's limit ourselves to polynomials。

## 导数的矩阵（The matrix of a derivative）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| In this coordinate system, the derivative is described with an infinite matrix that's mostly full of zeros, but which has the positive integers counting down on the offset diagonal. | 在这个坐标系里，导数由一个无穷矩阵描述：它几乎全是零，但有一条错开一格的对角线，正整数在上面逐级递减。 |
| We'll talk about how you could find this matrix in just a moment, but the best way to get a feel for it is to just watch it in action. Take the coordinates representing the polynomial $x^3 + 5x^2 + 4x + 5$, then put them on the right of the matrix. | 稍后我们会谈你怎么找到这个矩阵，但领会它最好的办法，就是直接看它运行。取表示多项式 $x^3 + 5x^2 + 4x + 5$ 的坐标，放到矩阵右边。 |
| The only term that contributes to the first coordinate of the result is $1\cdot4$, which means the constant term in the result will be $4$. This corresponds to the derivative of $4x$. The only term contributing to the second coordinate of our result is $2\cdot5$, which means the coefficient in front of $x$ in our derivative is $10$. This corresponds to the derivative of $5x^2$ being $10x$. Similarly, the third coordinate of the result comes down to taking $3\cdot1$, which corresponds to the derivative of $x^3$ being $3x^2$. And after that, it will be nothing but $0$'s. | 对结果第一个坐标有贡献的只有 $1\cdot4$ 这一项，也就是说结果中的常数项是 $4$，这对应 $4x$ 的导数。对结果第二个坐标有贡献的只有 $2\cdot5$ 这一项，也就是说导数中 $x$ 前面的系数是 $10$，这对应 $5x^2$ 的导数是 $10x$。类似地，结果的第三个坐标归结为取 $3\cdot1$，对应 $x^3$ 的导数是 $3x^2$。再往后，就全是 $0$ 了。 |
| What makes this possible is that the derivative is linear, and for those of you who like to pause and ponder, you can construct this matrix by taking the derivative of each basis function, and putting the coordinates of the results in each column. | 让这一切成为可能的，是导数的线性性；对喜欢停下来琢磨的人，你可以对每个基函数求导、把结果的坐标放进各列，从而构造出这个矩阵。 |
| The upshot here is that matrix-vector multiplication and taking a derivative, which at first seem like completely different animals, are really members of the same family. In fact, most of the concepts we've talked about in this series with respect to vectors as arrows in space, things like the dot product of eigenvectors, have direct analogs in the world of functions, though you might see them go by different names like inner product and eigenfunction. | 这里的要点是：矩阵-向量乘法和求导数，起初看似完全不同的两类动物，其实真真切切是同一个家族的成员。事实上，本系列里我们针对「空间中的箭头」谈过的大多数概念——比如特征向量的点积之类——在函数的世界里都有直接的对应物（analog），只是你可能看到它们顶着别的名字，比如内积（inner product）和特征函数（eigenfunction）。 |

## 抽象向量空间（Abstract vector space）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| To return to the question of what is a vector, the point is that there are other vector-ish things in math. As long as you're dealing with a set of objects where there's a reasonable notion of scaling and adding, whether that's a set of arrows in space, lists of numbers, functions, or some other crazy thing you choose to define, all the tools developed in linear algebra regarding vectors, linear transformation and all that stuff, should be able to apply. | 回到「向量是什么」的问题，要点在于：数学里还有其他带向量味的东西。只要你处理的是一组对象、其上存在合理的缩放与相加概念——无论那是空间中的箭头、数字列表、函数，还是你选择定义的某个别的疯狂东西——线性代数中围绕向量、线性变换等发展出来的所有工具，都应该能够适用。 |
| Tools: Linear transformations, Null space, Eigenvectors, Dot products | （图注）工具：线性变换、零空间、特征向量、点积 |
| Take a moment to imagine yourself as a mathematician developing the theory of linear algebra, you want all your definitions and discoveries to work for all of these vector-ish things in full generality. | 花点时间想象自己是发展线性代数理论的数学家：你要让你所有的定义与发现在完全的一般性下，对所有这些带向量味的东西都成立。 |
| A set of vector-ish objects, like the set of all points in 3d space, the set of all lists of 4 numbers, the set of all finite polynomial functions, etc., is called a "vector space". What you as the mathematician might do is say "Hey everyone, I don't want to think about all the different types of crazy vector spaces that y'all might come up with", so you establish a list of rules that vector addition and scaling have to abide by in order for the results that you-the-mathematician discover to apply to someone's chosen definition of vectors. | 一组带向量味的对象——比如三维空间中所有点的集合、所有 4 数列表的集合、所有有限多项式函数的集合等等——叫作一个「向量空间」（vector space）。身为数学家的你，可能会说：「各位，我可不想把你们可能想出来的各种疯狂向量空间都想过一遍。」于是你立下一份规则清单，规定向量加法与缩放必须遵守，这样你这位数学家发现的结论才能应用于别人选定的向量定义。 |
| These rules are called "axioms", and in the modern theory of linear algebra there are 8 axioms that any vector space must satisfy. They are all shown in the image above for anyone who wants to pause and ponder, but basically it's a checklist to be sure the notions of vector addition and scalar multiplication are reasonable. | 这些规则叫作「公理」（axioms），在现代线性代数理论中，任何向量空间都必须满足 8 条公理。想停下来琢磨的人可以在上面的图里看到全部；但基本上，它是一份检查清单，用来确保向量加法与标量乘法这两个概念是合理的。 |
| These axioms are not so much fundamental rules of nature as they are an interface between you, the mathematician discovering results, and other people who may want to apply those results to new sorts of vector spaces. | 这些公理与其说是自然界的根本法则，不如说是一个接口（interface）：一端是发现结论的你这位数学家，另一端是想把那些结论应用于新型向量空间的其他人。 |
| If, for example, someone defines some crazy type of vector space, like the set of all pi creatures with some definitions of adding and scaling pi creatures, these axioms are like a checklist of things they need to verify about those definitions before they can start applying the results of linear algebra. | 比如说，如果有人定义了某种疯狂的向量空间——比如全体 pi 生物的集合，配上某种「pi 生物相加与缩放」的定义——这些公理就像一份清单，列出在开始应用线性代数的结论之前，他们需要就这些定义验证的事项。 |
| You as the mathematician never have to think about all the possible crazy vector spaces people might define; you just have to prove your results in terms of these axioms, so anyone whose definitions satisfy these axioms can happily apply your results. As a consequence, you'd tend to phrase all your results abstractly, which is to say only in terms of these axioms, rather than centering on a specific type of vector like arrows in space or functions. | 身为数学家的你，永远不必去想人们可能定义的所有疯狂向量空间；你只需用这些公理来证明你的结论，于是任何定义满足这些公理的人都可以放心地应用它们。因此，你会倾向于把所有结论表述得抽象——也就是说只用这些公理来表述——而不是围绕某一特定类型的向量（比如空间中的箭头或函数）来展开。 |
| For example, this is why every textbook you find will define linear transformations in terms of additivity and scaling, rather than talking about grid lines remaining parallel and evenly spaced, even though the latter is more intuitive and, at least in my view, more helpful for first-time learners. | 比如说，这就是为什么你找到的每一本教科书都会用可加性（additivity）与缩放来定义线性变换，而不谈「网格线保持平行且均匀分布」——尽管后者更直观，而且至少在我看来，对初学者更有帮助。 |
| So the mathematician's answer to "what are vectors?" is to ignore the question. In modern theory, the form they take doesn't matter: arrows, lists of numbers, functions, pi creatures, really they can be _anything_, so long as there is some notion of adding and scaling vectors that follows these rules. | 所以，数学家对「向量是什么？」的回答，是无视这个问题。在现代理论中，向量以什么形式出现并不重要：箭头、数字列表、函数、pi 生物，真的，它们可以是*任何东西*，只要存在某种遵守这些规则的向量加法与缩放概念。 |
| It's like asking what the number 3 really is. Whenever it comes up concretely, it's in the context of some triplet of things. But in math it's treated as an abstraction of all possible triplets of things that lets you reason about all those triplets using a single idea. | 这就像问「数 3 到底是什么」。它每次具体出现时，都处在某个三元组（triplet）事物的语境里。但在数学中，它被当作所有可能的三元组事物的抽象，让你能用单一的想法对所有那些三元组进行推理。 |
| It's the same with vectors, which have many embodiments, but math abstracts them all into a single intangible notion of a vector space. As anyone reading this series knows, we think it's better to begin reasoning about vectors in a concrete, visualizable setting like 2d space with arrows rooted at the origin. But as you learn more linear algebra, know that these tools apply much more generally and that this is the underlying reason why textbooks and lectures tend to be phrased, well, abstractly. | 向量也是一样：它有许多化身（embodiments），但数学把它们全都抽象成一个单一的、无形的向量空间概念。读过本系列的人都知道，我们认为更好的做法，是在一个具体、可视化的场景里开始对向量推理，比如在二维空间中、箭头根部扎在原点。但当你学到更多线性代数时，要知道这些工具的适用面广泛得多，而这正是教科书和讲课倾向于表述得——嗯——抽象的深层原因。 |

> [!note]- 词汇注释 · 抽象向量空间
> - **vector space** —— 向量空间。A set of vector-ish objects ... is called a "vector space"。
> - **axiom** /ˈæksiəm/ —— 公理。there are 8 axioms that any vector space must satisfy。
> - **checklist** /ˈtʃeklɪst/ —— 检查清单。basically it's a checklist。
> - **interface** /ˈɪntəfeɪs/ —— 接口。an interface between you and other people。
> - **embodiment** /ɪmˈbɒdimənt/ —— 化身、具体体现。vectors have many embodiments。
> - **intangible** /ɪnˈtændʒəbl/ —— 无形的。a single intangible notion of a vector space。
> - **triplet** /ˈtrɪplət/ —— 三元组。it's in the context of some triplet of things。
> - *not so much A as B* —— 与其说是 A，不如说是 B。not so much fundamental rules of nature as they are an interface。
