## 展开圆环（Unraveling rings）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| Focus on just one of those concentric rings, and let's call its radius $r$, which will be some number between $0$ and $1$. If we can find a nice expression for the area of one such ring, and if we find a nice way to add them all up, it might lead us to an understanding of the full circle's area. | 先只盯住其中一个同心圆环，设它的半径为 $r$——这是介于 0 和 1 之间的某个数。如果我们能为这样一个圆环的面积找到一个漂亮的表达式，再找到一个漂亮的方法把它们全部加起来，或许就能通向对整个圆面积的理解。 |
| This figure highlights a band of area formed from dividing the circle into concentric rings. | （图注）这张图高亮了把圆分割成同心圆环后形成的其中一条环形带。 |
| In the spirit of using what will come to be standard calculus notations, let's call the thickness of one of these rings "$dr$", which you can read as meaning "the tiny difference in radius from one ring to the next". In the drawing above, for example, $dr = 0.1$. | 本着使用日后会成为标准微积分记号的精神，我们把其中一个圆环的厚度叫作 "$dr$"，你可以把它读作「从一个圆环到下一个圆环，半径的微小差值」。比如在上面的图里，$dr = 0.1$。 |
| You should know that some people, perhaps even most people, would object to our using the notation $dr$ to represent a specific not-infinitely-small size like this, suggesting instead we use some other notation, like $\Delta r$. Well, it's my house and my rules, and I have my reasons, which we'll get into later. For now, I want you to think about $dr$ as simply being some number without worrying about what phrases like "infinitely small" would mean. | 你应当知道，有些人——甚至可能是大多数人——会反对我们用记号 $dr$ 来表示这样一个并非无穷小的具体尺寸，他们会建议改用别的记号，比如 $\Delta r$。不过，这是我的地盘，规矩由我定，而且我有自己的理由，后面会细说。眼下，我要你把 $dr$ 就当成某个具体的数来看待，不必操心「无穷小」这类说法到底意味着什么。 |
| **Which of the following is closest to the area of one of these rings with inner radius $r$ and thickness $dr$?** | （译注：原文此处是一道互动选择题，选项为 $\pi r^2 \cdot dr$、$2 \pi r^2 \cdot dr$、$2 \pi r \cdot dr$、$\pi r \cdot dr$，正确答案为第三个，解析见下。）**对内半径为 $r$、厚度为 $dr$ 的这样一个圆环，下列哪个数最接近它的面积？** |
| The area of the ring can be approximated as the area of a rectangle with a length of $2 \pi r$ and a height of $dr$. | （译注：上题的答案解析。）这个圆环的面积，可以近似成一个长为 $2 \pi r$、高为 $dr$ 的矩形的面积。 |
| You might imagine straightening out this ring, into a shape whose width is the inner circumference of the ring, which is $2 \pi r$. This is the very definition of $\pi$, relating the circumference of a circle to its diameter, or radius. The thickness of this unwrapped shape would be $dr$. | 你可以想象把这个圆环拉直，变成一个宽度等于该圆环内周长的形状，内周长是 $2 \pi r$。这正是 π 的定义本身：把圆的周长和它的直径（或半径）联系起来。这个展开后的形状，厚度就是 $dr$。 |
| This figure illustrates a concentric ring with radius $r$ straightened out. | （图注）这张图展示了半径为 $r$ 的同心圆环被拉直后的样子。 |
| If this unwrapped shape is meant to perfectly match the area of the ring, it will be approximately, but not exactly, a rectangle. Because the outer circumference of the ring will be slightly larger than the inner circumference, the bottom of our unwrapped shape will be slightly wider than the top. However, if we're comfortable beginning the exploration by only approximating the area of each small piece, we could consider this to be approximately a rectangle with a width of $2 \pi r$ and a height of $dr$. | 如果这个展开后的形状要和圆环面积完全一致，那它近似是、但不完全是一个矩形。因为圆环的外周长会比内周长略大一点，我们展开后的图形底部会比顶部略宽。不过，如果我们接受在探索之初只对每个小块的面积做近似，那就不妨把它当作一个宽 $2 \pi r$、高 $dr$ 的近似矩形。 |
| （译注：原文此处有一条长脚注：「You might hypothesize that the appropriate shape would be a trapezoid, but how would you be sure that those small diagonal edges are straight lines? It's not hard to intuitively justify but to make it rigorous you end up running into awkward questions about what we even mean by "area" in the first place. The kind of rectangular approximations this article is leading to, and the general tools like integration that emerge from it, end up being one path to rigorously defining what we mean by area.」） | （译注：脚注中译。）你可能会猜，合适的形状该是梯形；可你怎么保证那两条小小的斜边真是直线呢？凭直觉论证并不难，但要想严格化，你就会撞见一些尴尬的问题：我们说的「面积」究竟是什么意思。本文所引向的那类矩形近似，以及由它诞生的积分等一般性工具，最终恰恰是严格定义「面积」的途径之一。 |
| The difference between the true area of the ring and the area of the rectangular approximation introduces some small error. | 圆环的真实面积与矩形近似面积之间的差别，带来了一些小误差。 |
| This figure illustrates how approximating a ring's area as a rectangle gets better and better for smaller choices of $dr$. | （图注）这张图展示了把圆环面积近似成矩形时，$dr$ 取得越小，近似就越好。 |
| However, and this will be a key idea, _this error becomes tiny compared to the overall area when $dr$ is small_. In other words, approximating the area of each ring as $2 \pi r \cdot dr$ is wrong, but as we chop up the circle into finer and finer rings, it becomes less and less wrong. | 不过——这将是一个关键思想——当 $dr$ 很小时，*这个误差相对于整体面积会变得微不足道*。换句话说，把每个圆环的面积近似成 $2 \pi r \cdot dr$ 确实是错的，但当我们把圆切得越来越细，这个近似就变得越来越不那么错。 |
| （译注：原文此处有一条长脚注：「The astute among you may worry about whether the errors in our approximations accumulate too much. You might say "Sure, sure, the difference between the true area of a ring as $2 \pi r \cdot dr$ is only a small percentage of the area of that ring, and it only gets smaller as $dr$ approaches $0$, I get that. But how do we know that the sum of all these errors doesn't remain significant as $dr$ approaches $0$?" If you wanted to be more precise and rigorous, you would show that the size of this error for each ring is bounded by some constant times $(dr)^2$, while the total number of rings is about $\frac{R}{dr}$, so the total size of the aggregate error is bounded by some constant times $\frac{R}{dr} (dr)^2 = R \cdot dr$. So as $dr$ goes to zero, the full aggregate error will indeed go to zero with it.」） | （译注：脚注中译。）机敏的读者可能会担心：近似带来的误差会不会累积得太多？你也许会说：「懂懂懂，圆环真实面积与 $2 \pi r \cdot dr$ 之差只占该环面积的一小部分，而且随着 $dr$ 趋近 0 还会更小，这我明白。可我们怎么知道，当 $dr$ 趋近 0 时，所有这些误差加起来不会依然可观？」若要更精确严格，可以证明：每个圆环的误差大小被某个常数乘以 $(dr)^2$ 所限定，而圆环总数约为 $\frac{R}{dr}$，所以误差总和被某个常数乘以 $\frac{R}{dr}(dr)^2 = R \cdot dr$ 限定。于是当 $dr$ 趋于零时，误差总和确实随之趋于零。 |
| This figure illustrates small and smaller choices of $dr$ when dividing the circle into rings. | （图注）这张图展示了把圆分割成圆环时，$dr$ 的取值一步步变小的情况。 |

> - **concentric** /kənˈsentrɪk/ —— 同心的。concentric rings 同心圆环。
> - **ring** /rɪŋ/ —— 圆环。focus on just one of those concentric rings。

## 转到图上思考（Think to Graph）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| So you've broken up the area of this circle into all these rings, and you're approximating the area of each one as $2 \pi \cdot r \cdot dr$. You might think that actually adding all those areas together will be a nightmare, especially if you're seeking more accurate approximations with finer and finer divisions of the circle. However, being the bold mathematician that you are, you might have a hunch that taking this process to the utmost extreme may actually make things easier rather than harder. | 现在，你已经把圆的面积拆成了这一堆圆环，并且把每个环的面积都近似成 $2 \pi \cdot r \cdot dr$。你可能会想，真要把所有这些面积加起来简直是场噩梦，尤其如果你还想把圆分得越来越细、追求更精确的近似。然而，身为大胆的数学家，你也许会有一种预感：把这个过程推到极致，说不定反而会让事情变简单，而不是更难。 |
| The size of that inner radius for the rings ranges from $0$ for the smallest ring up to $1$ for the largest, spaced out by whatever thickness we chose for $dr$, like $0.1$. Or rather, they range from $0$ up to $1 - dr$, but as $dr$ gets smaller that upper bound looks more and more like $1$. | 这些圆环的内半径，从最小圆环的 0 一直取到最大圆环的 1，彼此之间隔着我们所选的 $dr$ 厚度，比如 0.1。更准确地说，它们的范围是从 0 到 $1 - dr$，不过随着 $dr$ 越来越小，这个上界看上去就越来越接近 1。 |
| This figure shows how the area of the circle is equal to the sum of rings' areas, where the area of each ring is given by $2 \pi \cdot r \cdot dr$. | （图注）这张图展示了圆的面积如何等于各圆环面积之和，其中每个圆环的面积由 $2 \pi \cdot r \cdot dr$ 给出。 |
| To help draw what adding those areas looks like, think about adding together your rectangular approximations. A nice way to visualize this is to fit all those rectangles upright, side-by-side standing on a horizontal axis. We can think of this horizontal axis as representing all the values of $r$ ranging from $0$ up to $1$. | 为了画出把这些面积加起来是什么样子，不妨想想怎样把那些矩形近似拼到一起。一个不错的可视化办法，是让所有这些矩形竖直立起、肩并肩地站在一条水平轴上。我们可以把这条水平轴看作代表 $r$ 从 0 到 1 的所有取值。 |
| Each rectangle has a thickness of $dr$, which is also the spacing between different values of $r$, which is why they fit so snugly right together. The height of any rectangle above a value of $r$, is exactly $2 \pi \cdot r$. This is the circumference of the corresponding ring. | 每个矩形的厚度都是 $dr$，这也正是不同 $r$ 值之间的间距，所以它们才能严丝合缝地拼在一起。立在任一 $r$ 值上方的矩形，其高度恰好是 $2 \pi \cdot r$——也就是对应圆环的周长。 |
| This figure plots the radius of each of the circle's rings on a numberline, where the spacing between each radius is equal to $dr$. | （图注）这张图把圆的各个圆环的半径标在一条数轴上，相邻半径之间的间距等于 $dr$。 |
| **What is the height of the rectangle whose left corner touches $r=0.3$? Note:** This is the rectangle highlighted in the still figure above. | （译注：原文此处是一道互动选择题，选项为 $6 \pi$、$\frac{3}{10} \pi$、$2 \pi$、$\frac{3}{5} \pi$，正确答案为第四个，解析见下。）**左下角落在 $r=0.3$ 处的那个矩形，高度是多少？注：**即上面静态图里高亮的那个矩形。 |
| Substituting the value of the inner radius $0.3$ into the formula for the circumference of a circle $2 \pi r$ calculates the height of the rectangle to be $\frac{3}{5} \pi$. | （译注：上题的答案解析。）把内半径 $0.3$ 代入圆周长公式 $2 \pi r$，算得矩形高度为 $\frac{3}{5} \pi$。 |
| A nice way to think of this setup is to draw the graph of $2 \pi r$, which is this straight line with a slope of $2 \pi$. Each of these rectangles extends up to the point where it just barely touches the graph. | 理解这幅图景的一个好办法，是画出 $2 \pi r$ 的图像——一条斜率为 $2 \pi$ 的直线。每个矩形都向上延伸，直到恰好碰到这条图像为止。 |
| The unraveled rings under the graph of $2 \pi r$. | （图注）展开后的圆环，落在 $2 \pi r$ 的图像之下。 |
| Again, we're being approximate here. Each of those rectangles only approximates the area of their corresponding ring from the circle. But remember, the approximation of $2 \pi \cdot r \cdot dr$ for the area of each ring will get less and less wrong as the size of $dr$ gets smaller and smaller. | 再强调一次，我们这里做的仍是近似。每个矩形只是对圆上对应圆环面积的近似。但请记住，用 $2 \pi \cdot r \cdot dr$ 近似每个圆环的面积，随着 $dr$ 越取越小，误差会越来越小。 |

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| Finding the area represented by this integral function is genuinely hard. | In that playful spirit, if you're lucky, here's something you might notice. ## 基本定理（Fundamental Theorem）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| We care about derivatives because they help us solve problems, and in our little exploration here we have a slight glimpse of one way they're used: They are the key to solving integral problems; problems that require finding an area under a curve. When you gain enough familiarity with computing derivatives, you'll be able to look at a situation like the one below, where you don't know what a function is, but you do know that its derivative should be $x^2$, and from that reverse engineer what the function must be. | 我们在乎导数，是因为它们帮我们解决问题；而在这趟小小的探索里，我们已经稍稍瞥见了它们的一种用法：导数是求解积分问题的钥匙——所谓积分问题，就是要求出曲线下方面积的问题。等你对计算导数足够熟练，你就能面对这样一种局面：你不知道某个函数是什么，但你知道它的导数应当是 $x^2$，并由此反推出这个函数必然是什么。 |
| This back and forth between integrals and derivatives, where the derivative of the function for an area under a graph gives you back the function defining the graph itself, is called the _fundamental theorem of calculus_. It ties together the two big ideas of integrals and derivatives, and shows how in some sense, each one is the inverse of the other. | 积分与导数之间的这种来回往复——「图像下方面积」这个函数的导数，会把定义图像本身的那个函数还给你——叫作*微积分基本定理*（fundamental theorem of calculus）。它把积分与导数这两大思想系在一起，并表明在某种意义上，两者互为逆运算。 |
| This figure illustrates the property of $\frac{dA}{dx}$ for a more general function $f(x)$. | （图注）这张图针对更一般的函数 $f(x)$，展示了 $\frac{dA}{dx}$ 的这一性质。 |
| All of this is only a high level view. What follows in this series are the details for both these big ideas, and more. And let me reiterate, at all points in this series I want you to feel like you could have invented calculus yourself, that if you drew the right pictures and played with each idea in the right way, all of the formulas, rules, and constructs could pop out quite naturally. | 以上都只是宏观概览。接下来这个系列会给出这两大思想各自的细节，以及更多内容。让我再重复一遍：我希望你在整个系列的每一刻都觉得，微积分本可以由你自己发明——只要你画出了对的图、以对的方式摆弄每个想法，所有这些公式、法则和构造都会相当自然地蹦出来。 |

## 彩蛋：dx 到底是什么？（Bonus section: So what is dx anyway?）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| Calculus is littered with expressions like $dA$, $dr$, $dx$, etc, which show up in the notation for both derivatives and integrals. Despite their front-and-center role, there is a surprising amount of ambiguity and conflicting instruction on what these terms really mean. | 微积分里到处都是 $dA$、$dr$、$dx$ 这类表达式，它们同时出现在导数和积分的记号里。尽管地位如此核心，关于这些项到底意味着什么，却存在着出人意料的含糊与彼此冲突的说法。 |
| In this chapter I encouraged you to think of $dr$ as the difference in the radius of our circles from one to the next, prompting you to take a very literal interpretation and imagine an actual number, like $dr = 0.01$. Likewise, I encouraged you to think of $dA$ as an actual number, specifically the amount that the area under a graph changes as we nudge the right endpoint by $dx$, again an actual number. A more common convention would be to call these changes $\Delta r$, $\Delta A$, and $\Delta x$, reserving the $dx$ notation for changes that are "infinitely small". | 在这一章里，我鼓励你把 $dr$ 想成相邻两个圆的半径之差，引导你采取一种非常字面的理解，去想象一个真实的数，比如 $dr = 0.01$。同样，我鼓励你把 $dA$ 也想成一个真实的数——具体说，就是当我们把右端点轻推 $dx$ 时，图像下方面积变化了多少，这同样是一个真实的数。更常见的约定是把这些变化量叫作 $\Delta r$、$\Delta A$ 和 $\Delta x$，而把 $dx$ 这个记号留给「无穷小」的变化。 |
| Infinitely small? | （译注：原文此处是一幅 PiCreature 思想泡泡图，泡泡里写着这句话，非正文段落。）「无穷小？」 |
| The issue with this is that phrases like "infinitely small" and "infinitesimal" run the risk of, well, not actually making any sense. What is an infinitesimal change? Is it a number? If so, which number? It shouldn't be zero, but it also shouldn't be anything bigger than zero. If it's not a number then what is it, exactly? | 问题在于，「无穷小」「无限小量」这类说法有可能压根就没有任何意义。什么是无穷小的变化？它是一个数吗？如果是，是哪个数？它不该是零，可也不该是任何大于零的东西。如果它不是数，那它到底是什么？ |
| In acknowledgment of this conundrum, one common sentiment is to encourage students not to take this notation too seriously. The typical dictum is that derivatives, despite being written as $\frac{df}{dx}$, are not really fractions, and that integrals, written as $\displaystyle \int f(x) dx$, are not really sums, despite the fact that the symbol $\int$ literally derives from the letter "S" for "Sum". Instead, the student is told, each of these constructs has a separate more complicated definition. But! If a student wishes for loose intuition before stepping up their game to a real analysis class, they might find it helpful to think of $dx$ as an infinitely small change in $x$, even if that phrase doesn't _quite_ make sense. It is, after all, only intuition. | 为了回应这个难题，一种常见的态度是劝学生别把这个记号太当回事。典型的教条是：导数虽然写作 $\frac{df}{dx}$，但并不是真正的分数；积分虽然写作 $\displaystyle \int f(x) dx$，也并不是真正的求和——尽管符号 $\int$ 确实源自代表「Sum（和）」的字母 S。学生被告知，这两种构各有各的、更复杂的独立定义。但是！如果一个学生想在进入实分析课程、升级自己的装备之前先要一点松散的直觉，那么把 $dx$ 想成 $x$ 的一个无穷小变化也许会有帮助，哪怕这个说法并不*完全*说得通。毕竟，那只是直觉而已。 |
| To my taste, this sentiment is well-intentioned but ultimately misleading. The rigorous underpinnings of calculus only ever involve real, finitely sized numbers, often followed by questions of what happens as these values get closer to 0. The proposal of this series is that when we interpret the notation of calculus, we can do so in a way which maps closely to this rigorous underpinning. If you're anything like I am, this actually makes calculus much more tangible and understandable than any "intuition" resting on the idea of an infinitesimal. | 在我看来，这种态度用心良苦，但终究会误导人。微积分严格的根基自始至终只涉及真实的、有限大小的数，随后再问当这些值越来越接近 0 时会发生什么。本系列的提议是：当我们解读微积分的记号时，可以采用一种与这个严格根基紧密对应的方式。如果你和我是一路人，你会发现这样做其实让微积分比任何立足于「无穷小量」概念的「直觉」都更切实、更好懂。 |
| In a nutshell, here's the philosophy for this series: When you see expressions with terms like $dx$, think of those terms as representing actual small numbers, say $dx = 0.001$. When you do this, the equations you write will become wrong, but they get less wrong as the choice for $dx$ approaches $0$. Think of the circular rings from earlier in this chapter. If you thought of $dr$ as an actual number, say $dr = 0.001$, none of these rings actually had an area of $2\pi r dr$. However, the percent error between the true area and $2\pi r \cdot dr$ gets smaller and smaller as the choice for $dr$ does as well. | 一句话概括，这就是本系列的哲学：当你看到含 $dx$ 这类项的表达式，就把这些项想成代表实际的小数，比如 $dx = 0.001$。这样做时，你写下的等式会是错的，但随着 $dx$ 的取值趋近 0，它们会变得越来越不那么错。想想本章前面那些圆环。如果你把 $dr$ 想成一个真实的数，比如 $dr = 0.001$，那么没有任何一个圆环的面积真的等于 $2\pi r dr$；然而，真实面积与 $2\pi r \cdot dr$ 之间的百分比误差，会随着 $dr$ 一同越取越小。 |
| There are more details and rules of thumb for _how_ you replace terms like $df$ and $dx$ with small numbers, and we'll address them as the series unfolds, but that's the main idea. | 关于*如何*用小数替换 $df$、$dx$ 这类项，还有更多细节和经验法则，随着系列展开我们会逐一讨论；但主旨就是如此。 |
| I mention all this now so that you know this is not the universal convention; again finite changes are more typically written with the greek letter $\Delta$, as in an expression like $\Delta x = 0.001$. I also want to assure you that the spirit of this notational philosophy is not to compromise rigor for intuition, but to do the exact opposite. | 我现在把这些讲清楚，是为了让你知道这并不是通行世界的约定；再说一遍，有限的变化量更常见的写法是用希腊字母 $\Delta$，比如 $\Delta x = 0.001$ 这样的表达式。我也想让你放心：这套记号哲学的精神，绝不是用严格性去换取直觉，而恰恰相反。 |
| And now, without further philosophical delay, let's dive into what exactly a derivative is. | 现在，哲学铺垫到此为止，让我们一头扎进正题：导数究竟是什么。 |
