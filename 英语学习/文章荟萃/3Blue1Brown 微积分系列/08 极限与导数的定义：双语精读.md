| To be honest, the idea of a limit is not really anything new. If you know what the word "approach" means you pretty much already know what a limit is, you could say the rest is a matter of assigning fancy notation to the intuitive idea of one value that just gets closer to another. But there are a number of reasons to dive deeper into this topic. | 说实话，极限这个想法其实并不算什么新东西。如果你知道「趋近」这个词是什么意思，你基本上就已经知道极限是什么了；可以说，剩下的不过是给「一个值不断接近另一个值」这个直觉想法配上一套花哨的记号。但有若干理由值得我们在这个话题上再深挖一层。 |

## 导数的正式定义（Formal Definition of the derivative）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| Let's take a look at the formal definition of the derivative. As a reminder, when you have some function $f(x)$, to think about the derivative at a particular input, maybe $x=2$, you start by imagining nudging that input by some tiny $dx$, and looking at the resulting change to the output, $df$. | 我们来看导数的正式定义。提醒一下：当你有某个函数 $f(x)$，要思考它在某个特定输入（比如 $x=2$）处的导数，你先想象把那个输入轻推一个微小的 $dx$，再看输出由此产生的变化 $df$。 |
| The ratio $df/dx$, which can nicely be thought of as the rise-over-run slope between the starting point on the graph and the nudged point, is almost what the derivative is. The actual derivative is whatever this ratio approaches as $dx$ approaches $0$. | 比值 $df/dx$——可以很自然地想成图像上出发点与被轻推点之间的「纵变除以横变」斜率——已经几乎就是导数了。真正的导数，是这个比值在 $dx$ 趋近 $0$ 时所趋近的值。 |
| Just to spell out what is meant here, that nudge to the output "$df$" is is the difference between $f$ at the starting-input plus $dx$ and $f$ at the starting-input; the change to the output caused by the nudge $dx$. | 把这里的含义说清楚（原文 is is 中重复的 is 系笔误，照录）：对输出的那个轻推「$df$」，就是 $f$ 在「起始输入加 $dx$」处的值与 $f$ 在起始输入处的值之差；也就是轻推 $dx$ 引起的输出变化。 |
| To express that you want to find what this ratio approaches as $dx$ approaches $0$, you write "lim", for limit, with "$dx$ arrow $0$" below it. | 要表达「你想求这个比值在 $dx$ 趋近 $0$ 时趋近于什么」，你就写下「lim」——代表极限（limit）——并在它下面写上「$dx$ 箭头指向 0」（"$dx$ arrow $0$"）。 |
| Now, you'll almost never see terms with a lowercase $d$, like $dx$, inside a limit like this. Instead the standard is to use a different variable, like $\Delta x$ (delta $x$), or commonly "$h$" for some reason. | 到了这一步，你几乎永远不会在这种极限里看到带小写 $d$ 的项，比如 $dx$。取而代之的标准做法，是换用一个不同的变量，比如 $\Delta x$（delta $x$），或者不知为何大家惯用的「$h$」。 |
| The way I like to think of it is that terms with this lowercase $d$ in the typical derivative expression have built into them the idea of a limit, the idea that $dx$ is supposed to eventually approach $0$. | 我喜欢的理解方式是：典型导数表达式里带这个小写 $d$ 的项，已经把「极限」的想法——$dx$ 终究要趋近 $0$ 的想法——内建在其中了。 |
| So in a sense this lefthand side "$df/dx$", the ratio we've been thinking about in the past lessons, is just shorthand for what the righthand side spells out in more detail, writing out exactly what we mean by $df$, and writing out this limit process explicitly. And that righthand side is the formal definition of a derivative, as you'd commonly see it in any calculus textbook. | 所以从某种意义上说，左手边这个「$df/dx$」——我们过去几课一直在想的那个比值——只是右手边更详细展开内容的速记：它把 $df$ 的含义精确写出来，把这个极限过程显式地写出来。而那个右手边，就是你在任何微积分教科书里都会常见到的导数正式定义。 |
| The formal derivative definition. Notice that nothing about this definition references the idea of an "infinitely small" change. | （图注）导数的正式定义。注意，这个定义里没有任何地方引用「无穷小」变化的想法。 |

> [!note]- 词汇注释 · 导数的正式定义
> - **formal** /ˈfɔːml/ —— 正式的、形式上的。the formal definition of the derivative。
> - **lowercase** /ˈləʊəkeɪs/ —— 小写的。terms with a lowercase $d$。
> - **shorthand** /ˈʃɔːthænd/ —— 速记、简略说法。is just shorthand for what the righthand side spells out。
> - **explicitly** /ɪkˈsplɪsɪtli/ —— 显式地、明确地。writing out this limit process explicitly。
> - **spell out** —— 说清楚、逐字逐句写出。Just to spell out what is meant here。
> - **righthand / lefthand** —— 右边的 / 左边的。this lefthand side、that righthand side。
> - *as a reminder* —— 提醒一下。As a reminder, when you have some function。
> - *have built into* —— 内建于、内置。have built into them the idea of a limit。

## 拒绝「无穷小」的牢骚（No infinitely small rant）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| Now, if you'll pardon me for a small rant here, I want to emphasize that nothing about this righthand side references the paradoxical idea of an "infinitely small" change. The point of limits is to avoid that. | 现在，请原谅我在这里发一小通牢骚：我要强调，右手边没有任何地方引用「无穷小」变化这个自相矛盾的想法。极限的意义正在于避开它。 |
| This value $h$ is the exact same thing as the "$dx$" I've been referencing throughout the series. It's a nudge to the input of $f$ with some non-zero, finitely small size, like $0.001$, that we're analyzing for arbitrarily small choices of $h$. In fact, the reason people introduce a new variable name into this formal definition, rather than just using $dx$, is to be clear that these changes to the input are ordinary numbers that have nothing to do with infinitesimals. | 这个值 $h$，与我贯穿整个系列一直说的「$dx$」是同一个东西。它是对 $f$ 的输入的一个轻推，有非零的、有限小的大小，比如 $0.001$，而我们要分析的正是 $h$ 任意小的取值。实际上，人们在这个正式定义里引入一个新变量名、而不是直接用 $dx$，原因就是要讲清楚：输入的这些变化是普通的数，与「无穷小量」（infinitesimals）毫无关系。 |
| Infinitesimals are quantities formally defined in the hyperreal number system to solve calculus type problems. Defining the operations and properties of these infinitely small, but non-zero quantities, essentially treating them like entities, can be used to define the dual number system and evaluate the derivatives of functions programmatically. | （译注：原文脚注。）无穷小量是在超实数系（hyperreal number system）里为求解微积分类问题而正式定义的量。定义这些无穷小但非零的量的运算与性质——实质上把它们当作实体来对待——可以用来定义对偶数系（dual number system），并以程序方式计算函数的导数。 |
| Rather than interpret $dx$ as an "infinitely small change", as others like to do, I think you can and _should_ interpret $dx$ as a concrete, finitely small nudge, just so long as you remember to ask what happens as it approaches $0$. This method helps build a stronger intuition for where the rules of calculus come from and, more than that, it's not just some trick for building intuitions. Everything I've been saying about derivatives with this concrete-finitely-small-nudge philosophy is a translation of the formal definition of derivatives. | 与其他人喜欢做的那样把 $dx$ 解读成「无穷小的变化」相反，我认为你可以、而且_应该_把 $dx$ 解读成一个具体的、有限小的轻推——只要你记得问它趋近 $0$ 时会发生什么。这个方法有助于建立更强的直觉，明白微积分的法则从何而来；不止如此，它也不只是建立直觉的把戏。我用这套「具体有限小轻推」哲学讲过的关于导数的一切，都是导数正式定义的一种翻译。 |
| Long story short, the big fuss about limits is that they let us _avoid_ talking about infinitely small changes by instead asking what happens as the size of some change to our variable approaches $0$. | 长话短说，围绕极限大做文章的原因在于：它们让我们_避开_谈论无穷小的变化，转而去问「变量的某个变化的大小趋近 $0$ 时会发生什么」。 |

> [!note]- 词汇注释 · 拒绝「无穷小」的牢骚
> - **rant** /rænt/ —— 牢骚、激昂的长篇抱怨。pardon me for a small rant。
> - **pardon** /ˈpɑːdn/ —— 原谅、宽恕。if you'll pardon me。
> - **finitely** /ˈfaɪnaɪtli/ —— 有限地。some non-zero, finitely small size。
> - **arbitrarily** /ˌɑːbɪˈtrɛrəli/ —— 任意地。arbitrarily small choices of $h$。
> - **infinitesimal** /ˌɪnfɪnɪˈtesɪml/ —— 无穷小（量）。have nothing to do with infinitesimals。
> - **hyperreal** /ˌhaɪpəˈriːəl/ —— 超实的。the hyperreal number system（脚注）。
> - **quantity** /ˈkwɒntəti/ —— 量、数量。Infinitesimals are quantities。
> - *long story short* —— 长话短说。Long story short, the big fuss about limits...。

## 为什么我们在乎（Why we care）

| 原文 | 译文 |
|---|---|
| So why do we teach the limit notation and how does it help us formalize what it means for one value to approach another? | 那么，为什么我们要教极限记号？它怎样帮助我们形式化「一个值趋近另一个值」的含义？ |
| Maybe you are convinced that, on an intuitive level, the language and tools we have invented so far give us what we need to solve these types of problems. When thinking about small nudges to a function's input, as long as we imagine that nudge approaching zero, things usually work out. In the same way, we can zoom in on a point on a curve and convince ourselves that if we zoom in far enough the curve really starts to look straight. | 也许你已经信服：在直觉层面上，我们到目前为止发明的语言和工具，已经给了我们解决这类问题所需的东西。思考对函数输入的小轻推时，只要我们想象那个轻推趋近零，事情通常就能顺利解决。同样，我们可以放大曲线上的一点，并说服自己：只要放大得足够远，曲线真的开始看起来像直线。 |
| Zoom in on the exponential function $e^x$ at $x=0$ and the rate of change (slope) starts to look a whole lot like $1$. | （图注）在 $x=0$ 处放大指数函数 $e^x$，变化率（斜率）看上去就非常像 $1$。 |
| But, you may still have doubts and questions, as certainly some of the creators of calculus did. For example, just how far do we have to zoom in on a point before a function starts to look like its tangent line at that point? | 不过，你可能仍有疑虑和问题——当年微积分的某些缔造者肯定也有。比如，我们究竟要把一个点放大到多近，函数才开始看起来像它在该点的切线？ |
| If you are applying calculus, your primary concern is probably the accuracy of the approximation and you might play a cat and mouse game to choose a small enough $dx$ so that the approximation is is accurate enough to calculate a useful result. A mathematician, on the other hand, is playing a different game; they are interested in whether they can rig the cat and mouse game of choosing smaller and smaller values and getting a more accurate result so that they will _always win_. | 如果你是微积分的使用者，你首要关心的多半是近似的精度，你可能会玩一场猫鼠游戏，选一个足够小的 $dx$（原文 is is 中重复的 is 系笔误，照录），让近似精确到足以算出有用的结果。而数学家玩的是另一种游戏；他们感兴趣的是，能否操纵这场「选越来越小的值、得到更精确结果」的猫鼠游戏，让自己_永远获胜_。 |
| Even more than this, mathematicians care about ensuring all their statements have a precise and airtight meaning, which does not rest on visual intuition. As sensible as it might seem to say that this ratio approaches a certain value, and to draw a line through the graph whose slope approaches that of a tangent line, those seeking rigor insist on something better. In the next chapter, we'll take a look the epsilon-delta definition of limits, which finally puts this idea of "approaching" onto a firm foundation. | 比这更进一步，数学家还关心确保他们的所有陈述都有精确而严丝合缝的含义，不依赖视觉直觉。「这个比值趋近某个值」「画出一条斜率趋近切线斜率的直线」这些说法看似多么有理，追求严格性的人却坚持要更好的东西。下一章，我们会看极限的 epsilon-delta 定义（原文 take a look the 系笔误，应为 take a look at the，照录），它最终把「趋近」这个想法放到坚固的地基上。 |

> [!note]- 词汇注释 · 为什么我们在乎
> - **primary** /ˈpraɪməri/ —— 首要的。your primary concern。
> - **accuracy** /ˈækjərəsi/ —— 精度、准确性。the accuracy of the approximation。
> - **airtight** /ˈeətaɪt/ —— 严丝合缝的、无懈可击的。precise and airtight meaning。
> - **rig** /rɪɡ/ —— 操纵、做手脚。rig the cat and mouse game。
> - **sensible** /ˈsensəbl/ —— 有道理的、合理的。As sensible as it might seem。
> - **creator** /kriˈeɪtə/ —— 缔造者、创造者。some of the creators of calculus。
> - *cat and mouse game* —— 猫鼠游戏（你追我躲、反复较量）。play a cat and mouse game。
> - *work out* —— 顺利解决、行得通。things usually work out。
