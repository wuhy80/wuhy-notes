Lai Ching-te, who will take office as Taiwan’s president on May 20th, has **ambitious** (雄心勃勃的) plans for the island’s energy mix. He wants to push the proportion of **renewable** (可再生的) electricity production to 30% by 2030, up from 11% today. He also has plans to achieve **net-zero** (净零) carbon emissions by 2050. But some doubt he can fulfil these promises. **Blackouts** (停电) have been **plaguing** (困扰) the island. Can Taiwan, the source of over 60% of the world’s advanced **semiconductors** (半导体), avoid an energy **crunch** (短缺)?

Upon taking office in 2016, Tsai Ing-wen, Taiwan’s outgoing president, vowed to simultaneously reduce carbon emissions and **phase out** (逐步淘汰) carbon-free nuclear energy, which then stood at 12% of the mix (it now accounts for less than 7% of electricity generated). Ms Tsai and Mr Lai belong to a party that is **avowedly** (公开宣称地) anti-nuclear. While this task made **ideological** (意识形态的) sense, it has turned out to be a struggle. Liang Chi-yuan at Taiwan’s National Central University estimates that only a quarter of planned **windpower** (风力) projects were on schedule between 2017 and 2022.
